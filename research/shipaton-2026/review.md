# Shipaton 2026 — Next Gen competitive review

**Snapshot: September 25, 2026 session.** This is an independent assessment of public evidence, not official judging, community consensus, or a win-probability forecast. Submissions can change before the deadline.

## Coverage and limits

All 61 nonempty gallery pages were collected: **1,464 unique entries**, with a full public project page retrieved for each. A 62nd page returned no entries. The first web-tool response showed 1,456; the live gallery and completed crawl showed 1,464. **163 entries link to GitHub** somewhere in the project article. A GitHub link can point to a library rather than the complete app.

The full corpus was screened programmatically for track, student, platform, repository and RevenueCat evidence, followed by closer reading of selected candidates. **43 projects receive individual assessments below; 20 explicitly identify a Next Gen entry/path.** Other entries are indexed, not individually ranked. Keyword screening can miss entrants who provide student/category information only in private form fields.

Nine repository reviews supplemented the submission descriptions: Konvo, Guru, Veta, Sikurepi, Replix, StudyLoop, TenderVerdict (competition-branch documentation), FitWitness and CareBridge. These were static source/documentation spot checks, not complete audits. Selected screenshots were inspected. Full demo videos were not watched end to end, apps were not installed, and purchases/tests were not independently rerun. User, revenue, performance and device-test figures remain author-reported unless explicitly identified as a source observation.

## The rubric used

Next Gen evaluates four things: (1) a useful, interesting or original idea; (2) meaningful working-app progress evident in video and code; (3) thoughtful RevenueCat monetization; and (4) technical choices, product thinking and presentation. No numerical weights are published for these four items. The qualitative ratings below describe evidence strength, not official scores.

Active student status and a qualifying academic email are required. The entry needs a public, licensed source repository and a demo; no store release is required for Next Gen. Academic-email verification and actual category selection are not publicly confirmable here. See the [rules](https://revenuecat-shipaton-2026.devpost.com/rules). An organizer explicitly confirms that [RevenueCat Test Store is sufficient](https://revenuecat-shipaton-2026.devpost.com/forum_topics/44695-next-gen-eligibility-is-a-test-store-only-purchase-sufficient). A local unlock boolean is not the same evidence as a Test Store transaction.

## Source observations that changed the ranking

- **Guru:** [useDakshina.ts](https://github.com/sangamgautam1111/guru/blob/master/src/hooks/useDakshina.ts) sets success when no offering is available, then increments locally stored sponsorship totals. A separate [service](https://github.com/sangamgautam1111/guru/blob/master/src/services/RevenueCatService.ts) calls the actual SDK. Both facts matter; the fallback should not be counted as a payment.
- **Replix:** the inspected [repository](https://github.com/safwansyntropixs-bit/shipathon-app) contains real native/RevenueCat work, but no root license file was found. This is a present evidence gap, not an official disqualification finding.
- **CareBridge:** [revenuecat.ts](https://github.com/PHONGUIT22/CareBridge/blob/main/src/services/revenuecat.ts) implements SDK purchases and a separate local demo override. Premium functionality and purchase verification are different tests.
- **Veta:** [billing.dart](https://github.com/impacnari/Veta/blob/main/lib/data/billing.dart) uses returned offerings, purchase results and restored entitlements rather than a decorative paywall.
- **FitWitness:** [repository evidence](https://github.com/DominiqueAndrew/fitwitness/tree/main/evidence) documents a Test Store transaction and its limits. This remains published evidence from the project, not access to its private RevenueCat dashboard.
- **Konvo:** the public [README](https://github.com/matthewcycmb/konvo) documents a real missing-product launch bug, its fix, entitlement handling and build instructions; an MIT license is present. This is useful technical/product-care evidence already available to judges.

## What this means for Konvo

Assuming Matthew Chan’s Konvo entry is yours, its strongest advantage is the combination of a personal problem, a narrowly defined solution, a shipped product and reported real use. The page reports more than 1,500 downloads, $1,066 revenue and 300,000 organic views as of September 21. Those figures were not independently checked. They support usefulness and execution; Next Gen does not have a separate traction criterion.

Before the deadline, make those existing strengths easy to judge:

1. **Show the product immediately.** A real message reply, the absence of the unwanted feed surfaces, and the optional Screen Time flow should occupy the first part of the demo.
2. **Show the RevenueCat lifecycle.** Include a real sandbox purchase or restore and the resulting access change, with cancellation/error behavior explained in the repository. Distinguish Test Store, sandbox and production figures.
3. **Put the engineering lesson near the top.** The missing lifetime product and production-only paywall hang are unusually concrete proof of iteration. Explain the fix briefly and link the regression check.
4. **Make reproducibility easy.** Keep the licensed public repo, exact build prerequisites, relevant source paths and known limits prominent. The public README already does much of this.
5. **Tighten presentation.** The page currently says September 31, which is not a date. Keep only claims the video/source can substantiate and replace long motivational passages with product evidence. Explain Instagram-layout dependence and recovery in practical terms.

Your strongest competitors do not all compete on the same dimension. Guru has a more direct access-to-education impact story; Veta has a more distinctive visual metaphor; Sikurepi shows a broader complete workflow; TenderVerdict and FitWitness are meticulous about test-purchase evidence. Konvo can answer with demonstrated everyday usefulness, real deployment lessons and a clean, inspectable purchase flow.
